# Interest-tagged restock alerts remain Nigeria's open lane

No platform found in this research combines all of the "Biztom" feature set for small Nigerian sellers: a unified WhatsApp/Instagram/Facebook inbox, per-customer product-interest tags, automatic back-in-stock and price-change alerts, quiet-chat follow-up, in-chat orders and payments, and sales tracking. The pieces exist, but they sit in different products. **Bumpa** (₦5k–25k/month) owns store operations and inventory, **Loop56** is the closest AI-inbox rival (WA+IG+FB inbox, in-chat invoices and payment links, five Nigerian languages), and global inbox tools such as **Wati, respond.io, Zoko and SleekFlow** charge $49–$349/month plus message fees. **Back-in-stock and price-drop alerts** are mature, but almost only as Shopify plugins (Klaviyo, Swym, Stok) or enterprise tools (Charles). None of the African tools found advertises them. The white space is a Naira-priced, mobile-first tool that tags interest straight from DMs, and it works for sellers who have no Shopify store. It should send restock alerts that are honest and inventory-aware, and it should use Paystack or Flutterwave payment links rather than holding merchant funds. Two constraints shape the design as much as any feature does. From **1 October 2026** Meta charges Nigerian businesses about **₦14 per service or utility message and about ₦84 per marketing message**, and Instagram allows automated replies **only within 24 hours**. Nigerian law also bars false scarcity claims (FCCPA s.123) and requires express consent for direct marketing (NDPA/GAID 2025). A caveat on the brief itself: "Biztom" could not be verified publicly. The closest match is **Bizthom** (bizthom.com), a Nigerian AI commerce tool with WhatsApp cart follow-ups, and no public source describes its restock-tagging feature. Many primary pricing pages were blocked during research, so every price below needs re-checking before use.

## "Biztom" is probably Bizthom, and its restock feature is unverified

Two targeted searches for "Biztom" found no website, no press coverage and no app-store listing. They returned only generic Nigerian WhatsApp-selling guides and non-African back-in-stock plugins ([Techpoint](https://techpoint.africa/general/online-store-business-whatsapp-instagram-nigeria/), [Kanal](https://www.getkanal.com/whatsapp-features/back-in-stock-notifications)). The likely intended product is **Bizthom**, a Nigerian AI commerce tool whose public page mentions WhatsApp cart follow-ups ([Bizthom](https://bizthom.com/?amp=&amp=)). No source describes interest tagging or restock alerts under either spelling. This report therefore treats the feature set as the user described it, not as a verified competitor profile. Bizthom's pricing, founders, funding and user count are all unknown. The user should confirm the URL or social handle before benchmarking against it directly.

## Nigeria's field splits between store suites and AI chat assistants

Nigerian social-commerce tools fall into three groups. The first is store and operations suites (Bumpa, Catlog, Take App, Paystack Storefront). The second is newer AI chat-sales assistants (Loop56, Tradelynk, Revora, Musskart, Lura). The third is creator checkouts (Selar, Mainstack). The graveyard is instructive: **Kippa** shut agency banking and went silent after raising about $14.3M ([Launch Base Africa](https://launchbaseafrica.com/2025/08/18/founders-exit-website-down-the-unraveling-of-target-global-backed-kippa-that-raised-over-14m/)). **Sabi** left merchant tooling for commodity traceability ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/06/20/after-raising-38m-african-e-commerce-startup-sabi-lays-off-20-pivots-to-traceable-exports/)). **Chpter**, the Kenyan AI WhatsApp/IG sales tool that targeted Nigeria, was folded into Cloud9's business banking in August 2026, bringing about 4,500 merchants ([TechCabal](https://techcabal.com/2026/08/05/exclusive-kenyas-cloud9-acquires-chpter-less-than-a-year-after-founders-left/)). A standalone chat-commerce tool has struggled to survive on subscriptions alone. The ones that last tend to attach to money flows such as payments, credit or banking.

**Bumpa** is the market leader. It covers inventory, payments, Instagram DMs, WhatsApp orders, customer messaging, dispatch and analytics, all priced in Naira ([Bumpa](https://www.getbumpa.com/blog/bumpa-vs-shopify)). Its reported plans are Starter ₦5,000, Pro ₦10,000 and Growth ₦25,000 per month, billed quarterly or longer ([Bumpa support](https://support.getbumpa.com/support/solutions/articles/150000031628-bumpa-subscription-plans)). Newer "Scale" and "Premium" tiers added multi-location and staff management ([TechCabal](https://techcabal.com/2026/01/05/bumpa-milestone-2025/)), so the tier list is inconsistent across sources. Bumpa had **50k+ businesses and $20M GMV** at its $4M seed ([TechCabal](https://techcabal.com/2022/10/19/nigerian-social-commerce-startup-bumpa-raises-4-million-seed-round/)) and launched SME credit in May 2026 ([TechCabal](https://techcabal.com/2026/05/08/bumpa-and-vendorcredit-partner-to-launch-bumpa-capital/)). Its weak spot shows in app-store reviews: **withheld or delayed settlements, KYC-blocked withdrawals and slow support** ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/bumpa-manage-orders-easily/id1497638594?see-all=reviews&platform=iphone)). A competitor that never holds merchant money avoids that whole class of complaint.

**Loop56** is the most direct threat. It runs an AI sales assistant across Instagram, WhatsApp and Messenger in a unified inbox. The assistant recommends catalog items with images and prices, creates orders, calculates delivery fees, issues invoices and sends payment links in-chat, in **English, Pidgin, Yoruba, Igbo and Hausa**. The site claims 2,300+ Nigerian businesses ([Loop56](https://loop56.com/features/ai-sales-assistant)), and its pricing was not found. **Catlog** routes storefront orders into WhatsApp and issues invoice links, from a reported ₦2,350/month ([Catlog blog](https://blog.catlog.shop/catlogs-new-pricing/)). **Take App** is free to start and then about $50/month, which is expensive for Nigerian micro-sellers ([Take App](https://www.take.app/pricing)). **Paystack Storefront** is a free checkout and catalog tool with no inbox ([Paystack](https://support.paystack.com/en/articles/2132738)). Selar charges 4% + ₦50 per Naira sale with no monthly fee ([Nathan Ojaokomo](https://nathanojaokomo.com/blog/selar-alternatives)), which is a useful reference for sellers' tolerance of per-sale fees.

## Global inbox tools charge $49–$500 a month and bury the real bill

Global shared-inbox platforms have made the basics standard: a multi-agent inbox, tags and custom attributes, broadcasts, template management, flow builders, Shopify/WooCommerce sync and abandoned-cart flows. They sell these through tiered, multi-meter pricing. **Wati** runs from $59 to $279/month and adds roughly a 20% markup on Meta message fees. Users report bills about **5x the headline** and disputes over being charged after cancelling ([Chatarmin](https://chatarmin.com/en/blog/wati-pricing), [Flowcart](https://www.flowcart.ai/blog/wati-reviews)). TechCabal nevertheless rates it the most e-commerce-ready option for African merchants ([TechCabal](https://techcabal.com/2026/04/21/whatsapp-chatbots/)). **respond.io** holds broadcasts, workflows and AI back until its $159 Growth tier and meters monthly active contacts ([Chatarmin](https://chatarmin.com/en/blog/respond-io-pricing)). **SleekFlow** bills on five meters with a real floor of about $153/month ([BossBot](https://bossbot.uk/blog/sleekflow-pricing-review-2026)). **ManyChat** starts at $39 for 2,500 contacts, charges $29 extra for AI and scores 2.2/5 on Trustpilot, and mass comment-to-DM automation has led to Instagram bans ([Hack'celeration](https://hackceleration.com/labs/review/manychat)).

The India-born tools are the closest analogues for a price-sensitive SMB market. **Zoko** ($49.99/month + $0.015 per conversation) is reported to embed Paystack and Flutterwave links, though only one source says so ([TechCabal](https://techcabal.com/2026/04/21/whatsapp-chatbots/)). **DoubleTick** is mobile-first and lets a buyer send a photo that gets matched to a product and added to the cart ([Capterra](https://www.capterra.com/p/10003201/Double-Tick/)). **Interakt, AiSensy and Gallabox** cost about ₹1,000–3,500/month and are built around Razorpay/UPI ([respond.io](https://respond.io/blog/interakt-review), [Gallabox](https://gallabox.com/pricing)). **Charles** (Berlin) is the only inbox vendor found with native restock and back-in-stock reminders, and it is sold to enterprises on custom quotes ([Chatarmin](https://chatarmin.com/en/blog/charles-alternative)).

Meta itself is now the biggest competitor on AI replies. **Meta Business Agent**, available globally since 3 June 2026, answers questions, recommends products and qualifies leads across WhatsApp, Messenger and Instagram, and it is free to start ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/06/03/metas-ai-agent-for-whatsapp-business-is-now-available-globally/)). Generic AI FAQ answering will be hard to charge for. Native WhatsApp Pay does not reach Nigeria ([Infobip](https://www.infobip.com/blog/whatsapp-payments)), so in-chat payment there means Paystack, Flutterwave or Monnify links confirmed by webhook ([BossBot](https://bossbot.uk/blog/payment-links-ng)).

## Restock alerts convert best, and fair allocation is the untaken idea

Back-in-stock (BIS) messages are the highest-converting automation in published benchmarks. Omnisend reports **6.46% conversion and $9.14 revenue per BIS email**, the best of any automation ([Omnisend](https://www.omnisend.com/blog/email-marketing-statistics/)). A Klaviyo case study saw BIS SMS convert at 8.5% ([Klaviyo](https://www.klaviyo.com/blog/how-back-in-stock-text-messages-turn-waitlists-into-revenue)). No WhatsApp-specific or Nigerian BIS benchmark exists. Mature tools share a common core. Klaviyo notifies a set number of subscribers per restocked unit, oldest signups first, in timed batches while stock stays above a floor ([Klaviyo Help](https://help.klaviyo.com/hc/en-us/articles/115003872251)). Its price-drop flow triggers only for in-stock items and skips people who already bought ([Klaviyo Help](https://help.klaviyo.com/hc/en-us/articles/4404249033755)). Swym shows demand by variant ([Swym](https://www.getswym.com/features/back-in-stock-product-alerts)). STOQ and Timesact take pre-order deposits ([STOQ](https://www.stoqapp.com/), [Timesact](https://timesact.com/2025/05/20/partial-payments-on-preorders/)). WhatsApp-capable versions exist, including Stok and Klaviyo's February 2026 WhatsApp BIS forms, but all of them assume a Shopify or BigCommerce catalog ([Stok](https://apps.shopify.com/notifyme), [Klaviyo](https://community.klaviyo.com/product-updates-announcements-51/february-2026-product-updates-19034)).

Fair allocation is found only at large brands. Amazon's invite-only ordering gives selected requesters a **timed purchase window with a countdown**, and unselected requests roll over to the next restock ([Amazon](https://www.amazon.com/gp/help/customer/display.html?nodeId=GALNNSRHJU49GBVM)). Nike's SNKRS runs lottery draws and gives prior buyers "Exclusive Access" priority ([Nike](https://www.nike.com/launch/t/inside-snkrs-fairness)). None of the Shopify apps surveyed advertises stock holds for waitlisters. That is the idea to port into DM commerce. When three units arrive and 300 people are waiting, a seller can notify a few people per unit, hold one unit for each person notified until a stated time, and release the unit when the time runs out. That turns a blast that frustrates 297 buyers into an honest queue.

## Meta fees and Nigerian law set the design envelope

**Unit economics.** Meta moved to per-message pricing on 1 July 2025 ([Meta docs](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/whatsapp/pricing/updates-to-pricing/)). From **1 October 2026** it also charges for in-window service replies and utility messages. Nigerian press reports about **$0.0101 (≈₦14)** per chargeable service or utility message and **$0.062 (≈₦84)** per marketing message. Businesses without a payment method on file by 30 September stop getting service messages delivered ([Guardian NG](https://guardian.ng/breakingnews/meta-to-charge-%E2%82%A614-per-whatsapp-message-from-october/), [Sahara Reporters](https://saharareporters.com/2026/08/28/whatsapp-set-start-charging-businesses-customer-messages-october-1)). Aggregator rate cards give lower figures ($0.0516 marketing, $0.0067 utility) ([Ominiflow](https://ominiflow.com/whatsapp-api-pricing/nigeria)), probably from an earlier version of Meta's rate card. One secondary source reports **1,000 free service messages per number per month** ([WithinNigeria](https://www.withinnigeria.com/2026/08/29/whatsapp-business-charges-what-nigerian-companies-need-to-know-before-october-1/)). Aggregators also say Meta caps marketing templates at about two per user per day across all businesses ([AiSensy](https://m.aisensy.com/blog/meta-frequency-capping-for-whatsapp-marketing-messages/)). The consequences are concrete. Blasting 300 waitlisters at marketing rates costs about ₦25,000, more than a month of Bumpa Growth. Notifying five people per unit for three units costs about ₦1,260 as marketing, or about ₦210 if Meta accepts the alert as utility. Whether a requested restock alert counts as utility depends on Meta's categorisation, which was not verified. Adding promotional or scarcity wording makes a marketing classification more likely. The free WhatsApp Business app is not charged ([Legit.ng](https://www.legit.ng/business-economy/technology/1728475-which-whatsapp-users-pay-n14-message-what-metas-billing-means-nigerians/)), and Coexistence lets a seller keep the app number while connecting it to the Cloud API ([respond.io](https://respond.io/blog/whatsapp-business-account-price)).

**The Instagram 24-hour window.** Businesses can reply to an Instagram DM only within 24 hours of the user's last message. The HUMAN_AGENT tag extends this to 7 days, but only for genuine human replies, and nothing is permitted beyond 7 days ([Meta policy](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/business-messaging/messenger-platform/policy)). Instagram therefore cannot deliver a restock alert weeks later. Automated quiet-chat follow-up on Instagram has to fire inside 24 hours, and follow-ups between day 1 and day 7 must come from a person. Any Instagram lead that asks about an out-of-stock item has to be converted to WhatsApp or SMS consent while the window is still open.

**Scarcity and consent law.** FCCPA 2018 s.123 prohibits false or misleading representations about price and the "manner or conditions of supply". s.124 bars coercive or unfair tactics, and fines can reach 10% of turnover ([LawGlobal Hub](https://www.lawglobalhub.com/section-123-federal-competition-and-consumer-protection-act-2018/), [Law Kernel](https://lawkernel.ng/consumer-rights-in-nigeria-under-the-fccpa-2018/)). The FCCPC enforces actively. It shut five Kano warehouses over deceptive sales in November 2025 ([Nairametrics](https://nairametrics.com/2025/11/05/fccpc-shuts-down-five-textile-warehouses-in-kano-over-deceptive-sales-practices/)). The FTC, EU UCPD and UK CMA all specifically target fake low-stock claims and countdowns that do not expire ([FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/system/files/ftc_gov/pdf/P214800+Dark+Patterns+Report+9.14.2022+-+FINAL.pdf), [Ashurst](https://www.ashurstperkinscoie.com/en/insights/mind-the-nudge-the-dmccs-crackdown-on-manipulative-online-design/)). "Only 3 came in, 300 are waiting" is lawful only if both numbers are real and current. On consent, NDPA s.26 requires consent that is specific, informed and easy to withdraw ([KPMG](https://assets.kpmg.com/content/dam/kpmg/ng/pdf/nigeria-data-protection-act2023_kpmg-review.pdf)). GAID 2025, in force since 19 September 2025, requires **express consent for direct marketing** ([Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/nigeria/privacy-protection/1700306/gaid-2025-unpacked-series-ii-data-protection-officers-mandatory-consents-and-emerging-technologies)). WhatsApp separately requires explicit opt-in before any business-initiated template ([WhatsApp policy](https://whatsappbusiness.com/policy/)). WhatsApp's terms also ban general-purpose AI chatbots, so any bot must stay within business-specific use ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/10/18/whatssapp-changes-its-terms-to-bar-general-purpose-chatbots-from-its-platform/)). NCC rules on SMS do-not-disturb were not researched and need checking before SMS is used as a fallback.

## Feature priorities for a Nigeria-first build

The white space is specific. No surveyed product combines **interest tags captured from DMs**, **restock and price-change alerts that work without Shopify**, **fair allocation with holds**, **Naira pricing with message costs shown in advance**, and **payment links that never hold merchant funds**. The global players either require Shopify or cost too much, and the Nigerian players have not shipped the demand-capture layer as far as public sources show. Loop56 is closest and could add it quickly, so speed matters. The order below puts compliance and economics before features that are nice to have.

| Priority | Feature | Why it wins / constraint it answers |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Unified WA + IG + FB inbox on a mobile app, with Coexistence so sellers keep their existing WhatsApp number | Table stakes. Sellers run the business from their phones (DoubleTick, Bumpa) |
| 2 | One-tap or AI-extracted **interest tags** (product, variant/size, price asked, location) written from the chat | The core differentiator. Also feeds reorder demand data that Swym-style dashboards charge for |
| 3 | **Consent capture in the conversation**: "Want a WhatsApp alert when size 42 lands? Reply YES", logged with a timestamp, plus one-tap opt-out | Satisfies NDPA s.26/GAID express consent and WhatsApp opt-in. Handles the IG 24h window by moving the lead to WA or SMS while it is open |
| 4 | **Inventory-aware restock alerts**: N notified per unit in batches, oldest or past-buyer priority, and **timed holds that really expire** | Cuts ₦84 message spend by about 90%. Holds that genuinely lapse keep deadlines truthful under FCCPA s.123 |
| 5 | **Scarcity copy generated from live data only**: counts computed by the system, sellers cannot type inflated numbers, audit log kept | Makes every scarcity claim defensible to the FCCPC, CMA or FTC |
| 6 | Price-drop alerts limited to in-stock items, skipping buyers who already purchased | Mirrors Klaviyo. Avoids irrelevant marketing sends and frequency-cap waste |
| 7 | In-chat order builder + **Paystack/Flutterwave/Monnify payment links** + bank-transfer confirmation, with no custody of funds | Nigeria has no WhatsApp Pay. Avoids Bumpa's settlement and KYC complaints |
| 8 | **Message-cost meter and prepaid Naira wallet**: shows "this send costs ₦1,260 (15 × ₦84)" before sending, with a utility-template path for plain restock notices | Answers the #1 complaint about global tools (bills 5x the headline) and makes the Oct 2026 fees visible |
| 9 | **Quiet-chat follow-up** timed to each channel's rules: automated on IG within 24h, human-only for days 1–7, WA templates only for consented contacts | Recovers leads that would otherwise be lost without breaking Meta policy or paying for marketing templates the customer didn't consent to |
| 10 | Sales tracking tied to tags: revenue by product, waitlist-to-sale conversion, demand by variant and area | Turns chat history into buying and reorder decisions |
| 11 | Deposit-backed pre-orders and waitlists | Filters out non-serious buyers and funds restocks (STOQ/Timesact pattern) |
| 12 | Local-language replies (Pidgin, Yoruba, Igbo, Hausa) and photo-to-product matching | Parity with Loop56 and DoubleTick. Kept lower because Meta Business Agent gives basic AI replies away free |

Flat Naira tiers aimed at the ₦2k–25k/month band where Bumpa and Catlog sit, plus pass-through messaging from the wallet, would undercut Take App and the dollar-priced inbox tools. The cost of those tools shows up in both the pricing sources and the reviews.

## Conclusion

The competitive risk is not a better-funded inbox. It is that Loop56 or Bumpa adds interest tags and restock alerts on top of the seller base they already have. The durable advantage is therefore the design that makes alerts cheap, lawful and trusted, not the alert feature itself. That means consent captured in the chat, notifications sized to actual stock, holds that genuinely expire, and message costs shown in Naira before every send. Meta's October 2026 charges make that discipline a pricing advantage as well as good practice. A tool that spends ₦1,260 where a competitor blasts for ₦25,000 can offer a cheaper plan and still keep a margin. The unresolved points are Meta's categorisation of restock alerts (utility vs marketing), the actual Oct 2026 Nigeria rate card, and Bumpa's latest release notes. Confirm those three before committing the roadmap.
